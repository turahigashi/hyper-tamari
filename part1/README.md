# ChowStanley — a Lean 4 companion (Part I)

Machine-checked companion to

> *Evaluation orders on bracketings in the hyperoperation hierarchy.*

This is the **Part I** development, covering the rank-three material. The Part II
development, covering the hyperoperation hierarchy and the Stanley order above rank
three, is the root of this repository (`HyperTamari`); Theorem 7.1 of the paper is
formalised there,
because the covering machinery it uses lives there.

`./verify.sh` builds the library and runs the axiom audit. The audit **enumerates the
environment** rather than reading a hand-written list of names, and it exits non-zero if
any declaration depends on `Classical.choice` or on `sorryAx`.

Last run: **292 declarations — 105 axiom-free, 187 depending only on `propext` and
`Quot.sound`, 0 on `Classical.choice`, 0 on `sorryAx`.** The raw output is in
`logs/axiom-audit.txt`.

`Tree.ev` evaluates a tree at a list of leaf values, and `Tree.ev_set` proves that
evaluating at "leaf `i` carries `t`, every other leaf carries `2`" gives exactly
`PTree.val (T.points[i]) t`. So `Tree.stanleyLe_of_evalLe` is a statement about the
evaluation order itself, not about an auxiliary function.

## Proposition 4.1

`Tree.stanleyLe` is *defined* by the right-depth coordinates, which is Knuth's
definition of the Stanley lattice (exercise 28 of the draft of §7.2.1.6 of *The Art of
Computer Programming*). Proposition 4.1 identifies that with the Dyck-path height order
of Definition 2.5, and **is formalised**, in the `w` convention in which the paper
states it:

| Paper | Lean |
|---|---|
| **Proposition 4.1** | **`Tree.stDom_iff_stanleyLe`** |
| Definition 2.5 ($T\le_{St}U$ as path dominance) | `Tree.stDom` |
| the same in the postfix convention | `Tree.qDom`, `Tree.stDom_iff_qDom` |
| the height after the $j$th up-step of $q\,T$ is the $(j+1)$st right depth | `Tree.upHeights_eq_rseq_tail` |
| $p_j + e_j = 2(j+1)$ | `Tree.upPos_add_upHeights` |

## Lemma 4.4 and Lemma 4.5

Both are formalised, in `Cover.lean`. `StCover T U` is the order-theoretic cover — `T`
below `U`, not conversely, and no tree strictly between — quantified over all trees, and
`SwapDU T U` is Definition 2.5's word statement, that `w U` is `w T` with one occurrence
of `DU` replaced by `UD`.

| Paper | Lean |
|---|---|
| **Lemma 4.4** | **`Tree.stCover_iff_swapDU`** |
| **Lemma 4.5** (local form, as an equivalence) | **`Tree.stCover_iff_stRot`** |
| a cover raises exactly one right depth | `Tree.stCover_iff_raisesOne` |
| the surgery realising that raise | `Tree.raise`, `Tree.rseq_raise` |
| the move of Lemma 4.5 | `Tree.StRot`, `Tree.graftLeft` |
| 2.8 of Csákány–Waldhauser (`r` is injective) | `Tree.rseq_injective` |
| the word determines the tree | `Tree.qword_injective`, `Tree.wword_injective` |
| every `DU` in `w T` is a move | `Tree.exists_stRot_of_swap` |

The one ingredient the printed proof does not need is that raising one coordinate again
gives the sequence of a *tree*. It is supplied by surgery on `T` itself: `Tree.raise`
descends to the place where the raise happens and performs the move of Lemma 4.5 there,
so no correspondence between trees and Dyck words has to be built.

Witnesses: `Tree.stCover_three` (a cover exists), `Tree.swapDU_three` with
`wword_three_lower`/`wword_three_upper` (it really is `UDUD ↦ UUDD`), and
`Tree.not_stCover_bot4_top4` (not every comparison is a cover).

## The order is generated by its covers

`Tree.stanleyLe_iff_reflTransGen_stRot` proves that `T ≤ U` in the Stanley order exactly
when `U` is reached from `T` by a finite chain of the local moves. This is the step that
turns the Part II theorem "evaluation does not decrease along a chain of moves" into a
statement about the order.

## What is **not** formalised here

* The converse direction of Corollary 1.3, which is Theorem 7.1 — formalised in the
  Part II development as `StRot.eval_le_rank3`, over its own tree type.
* **The joins between the two developments.** Part I's `Tree` carries an assignment as a
  list; Part II's `LTree` carries the values at its leaves. Nothing here translates
  between them, so the equivalence of Theorem 1.1 is *not* a single kernel-checked
  statement: the forward implication is (`Tree.stanleyLe_of_evalLe`), and both
  ingredients of the converse are, but their composition is done on paper. For the
  same reason, the identification of the local move `Tree.StRot` proved here to be the
  Stanley cover with the move `StRot` on labelled trees, along which Part II proves
  Theorems 7.1 and 14.3, is made on paper.
* The equality classification along a Stanley cover at rank three (Corollary 7.2 of the
  paper).

The forward implication of Theorem 1.1 is machine-checked end to end in this
development. The two ingredients of the converse are machine-checked, one in each
development, but the translation between their tree representations is not, so the
equivalence is not a single kernel-checked statement.

## Non-degeneracy

A separation theorem is worthless if nothing satisfies its hypothesis, and an
implication is worthless if its antecedent is unsatisfiable. Both are checked:

* `Tree.evalLe_refl`, `Tree.stanleyLe_refl` — neither relation is empty.
* `Tree.ev_node_leaf_leaf`, `Tree.ev_T4`, `Tree.ev_U4`, `Tree.ev_set_check` — `ev`
  really computes exponentiation, and `ev_set` is confirmed numerically ($4^{16}$ on
  both sides).
* `Tree.not_evalLe_T4_U4`, `Tree.not_evalLe_U4_T4` — the two trees of Example 4.3 are
  genuinely incomparable in the evaluation order.
* `not_stanleyLe_T4_U4`, `not_stanleyLe_U4_T4` — and in the Stanley order too.

## The audit is a gate, not a report

`scripts/Audit.lean` throws (so `lake env lean` exits 1) when a `Classical.choice` or
`sorryAx` dependency appears, or when fewer than 100 declarations are found — the last
condition guards against the audit silently looking at the wrong window. This was
mutation-tested: planting one `theorem _ : (1:Nat) < 2 := by norm_num` (which pulls
`Classical.choice`) makes the audit fail with exit code 1.

Several sources of `Classical.choice` were found and removed while writing this
development. **None of them was mathematical**; each was a tactic reaching for a
classical lemma where a constructive one was available:

* `norm_num` applied to a numeric *inequality* (on an equality it stays clean; `omega`
  or `decide` discharges the same goals without it);
* `omega` used to close a *non-arithmetic* goal from a contradictory arithmetic
  hypothesis (`exact absurd h (by decide)` is axiom-free);
* `omega` used on a *conjunction* (`⟨by omega, by omega⟩` is clean);
* `List.countP_eq_zero`, which itself depends on choice — `Word.countP_eq_zero_of_all`
  proves the same thing by induction;
* `simp` used to discharge `if`-reductions and `Option` disequalities — `if_pos`,
  `if_neg` and `Option.some_ne_none` do the same work constructively.
